# Auto-Deleveraging

ADL is the backstop for the backstop: when the vault's inherited liquidation inventory threatens losses the insurance fund cannot absorb, the protocol force-closes the *most profitable, most leveraged counterparties* against that inventory — the standard perp-exchange socialization mechanism, implemented as a permissionless Dutch auction.

## Trigger & freeze

After every matching transaction, liquidation, unload, withdrawal, price refresh, and ADL step, the pair computes the vault's **expected loss** (what closing the inherited inventory at the mark would cost beyond the collateral seized with it). When it exceeds the fund's **free capital above its reserve** — the reserve is 20% of the [insurance target](insurance.md) on open interest, kept for the losses no bound can refuse — ADL goes pending:

- All trading and order creation freeze; position-holders' withdrawals freeze (flat users may still exit — their cash can't affect scores or margin).
- The trigger price and funding index are snapshotted — the whole auction ranks against this frozen book, so a keeper's off-chain sort and on-chain execution agree; the target side is the *opposite* of the vault's inventory (the winners).
- Hysteresis: pending state only clears once that capital could finish the *remaining* inventory at the edge of the unload band — min(5%, MMR) from the oracle, the same figure each batch closes to — so the walk that resumes can sell the rest inside the band without reopening the window, and the boundary can't flap on a tick.
- The unload walk spends only the fund's headroom above that reserve, so an unload can never open a window on its own — and a walk that starts past the trigger takes only fills at or better than the estates' fund-neutral price, which cost the fund nothing, leaving the rest to the auction. Once a window is open, matching is frozen along with everything else, so `executeAdl` is the only path that retires inventory until it clears. See [Liquidations](liquidations.md).
- A hard deadline: pending for **24 hours** without resolution → the pair [winds down](termination.md) at the live price (the settlement price is fixed on the spot and the settlement window opens; anyone finalizes once every position is settled). ADL cannot become a limbo.
- Both the auction and that deadline require a price the oracle currently attests — the stored-price fallback is refused on either. On a pair whose venue is shut, the two are blocked together and would otherwise unblock in the same block at reopen, racing over a pair the auction might still save. So the deadline is not the clock alone: the first price stored past the 24 hours arms it **30 minutes** ahead, and only then may anyone terminate. The window is set once and never extended — a per-price refresh would be a snooze button, since the [refresh bounty](fees.md) pays keepers to store prices every ~10 minutes — and it is cleared when the auction resolves, so a later window on the same pair starts with its own full runway.
- If opposing liquidations flip the vault's inventory to the other side mid-auction, the target side is re-pointed and the price/funding snapshot re-taken — but the 24-hour clock is deliberately *not* reset, so repeated flips can never stall termination.

**When the fund is at or below its reserve**, the capital above it is zero and the gate collapses to its strictest form: any paper loss on inherited inventory opens a window on the next fresh verdict, each batch is sized to close the whole inventory, and the window clears only once the inventory is gone or sits above water by more than the band. The vault meanwhile sells only at its neutral price or better, so nothing is realized at the fund's expense, and winners carry every loss beyond the seizures while the taker insurance fee sits at or near its cap and LP withdrawals stay blocked. Every dollar of fees or deposits above the reserve restores a dollar of headroom and moves the trigger off zero — there is no memory and no separate threshold at the reserve line. [Share pricing](insurance.md) reads the same figure: a deposit or redemption deducts the inventory's unrealized loss only as far as the capital above the reserve covers it, because beyond that the auction charges the remainder to winners rather than to the fund — so in this regime shares price flat at free capital, and a depositor funding the rescue is not charged for a loss the auction will not take from the fund.

## The auction

Anyone may call `executeAdl(winners[], priceUpdate)` with up to 25 candidates, sorted by descending **ADL score**:

```
adlScore = positionPnL(at snapshot price) / collateral
```

— profit-to-collateral, so a 10× levered winner outranks a 2× one at equal profit. Collateral for scoring **excludes deposits made during the current ADL window** (epoch-tagged at deposit time): topping up mid-auction protects your margin but cannot re-rank you out of the queue. A withdrawn-to-zero winner scores effectively infinite — pure-profit claims stand first in line.

Eligibility decays like a Dutch auction: the score threshold starts at **25×** and decays quadratically to ~0 over **10 minutes**. The worst offenders are executable immediately; by minute ten, any profitable counterparty is.

The decay measures time the auction could actually run. ADL needs a price the oracle currently attests, so across a closure the curve would otherwise descend against attempts nobody was able to make — and the first keeper at the reopen would find the gate wide open, free to deleverage any profitable account instead of the worst-ranked ones the curve exists to reach first. A stored price that ends a gap of at least **1 hour** — the same discontinuity threshold the [reopen-gap leg](margin.md#the-stress-move) of the pair's stress move learns from, set so the refresh bounty's cadence separates a shut venue from ordinary call sparsity — therefore restarts the decay at 25×. Restarting forfeits whatever the auction had already spent, which is at most the ten minutes; the trade is a clock that is a single instant rather than an accumulated one. The 24-hour termination clock is separate and never rewinds, so a pair that keeps failing to deleverage still resolves.

## Settlement

Winners close at the **average bankruptcy price of the dead estates** — the price at which the vault's inventory realizes exactly the collateral it seized, so the close is neutral to the fund whatever the winner's own entry was — or at the **mark**, whenever the mark sits on the fund's favorable side of that price. A window can outlive a recovery past the bankruptcy price (it releases only once the fund could finish the remainder at the band edge), and `executeAdl` is permissionless with a caller-chosen winner list, so settling there at the bankruptcy price would let a winner deleverage themselves better than market at the fund's expense. Taking the mark keeps the close never better than market for the winner and hands the recovery to the fund:

- Winners whose PnL at that price would be *negative* are skipped (never worse than break-even).
- Oversized winners are partially closed pro-rata.
- The winner realizes their **full PnL at that price** into their bucket, exactly as a maker filled by a vault unload does. The part of that PnL between the winner's entry and the estate's entry was already settled by the winner's earlier counterparties, so it is backed by the deposits ledger, not by the fund.
- The inventory slice settles through insurance against the liquidatees' entry, **price and funding legs together**, as on an unload fill. At the bankruptcy price the two legs net to the seizure (already in the fund) plus the funding accrued while the vault held the inventory, because the bankruptcy price already embeds the estate's pre-liquidation funding; at a better mark the loss is smaller by the distance and the fund keeps the rest of the earmark. The fund can therefore only fall short by that holding-window carry: what it cannot pay is **withheld from the winner's funding income** — never from their price PnL — and reported in the `UserAdld` event. A shortfall beyond that (a seizure that was capped at the deposits ledger) is recorded as `deficit` and [winds the pair down](termination.md). Each slice is funded from its own seizure earmark plus the fund's free capital, so within a batch the highest-scored winners are paid in full and any withholding lands on the later ones.

A batch closes **only as much inventory as restores the fund**, never the whole aggregate because one winner happened to be large enough to cover it. The size is computed up front from the gap between what finishing the inventory at the band edge would cost and the fund's free capital above its reserve: each unit closed removes its share of that cost, and lowers the capital by the funding accrued while the vault held that unit and by the executor's cut of what it averts, so every leg sets the figure. Whatever the batch does not need stays with the winner; if no size restores health, the auction runs to its eligible size and the timeout or a recorded `deficit` resolves it.

The executor earns **0.1% of averted bad debt**, debited from the insurance fund's free capital (the earmark left against unclosed inventory never pays it) and accrued to its claimable balance (`claimFees()`) — so the reward is bounded by what the exposure actually required. A batch that settles at the mark averts nothing and pays nothing.
