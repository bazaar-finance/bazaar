# Insurance Fund

Each pair has its own insurance fund, held inside the pair contract and owned by **share-based LPs** — anyone can capitalize any market and earn its fee flow. There is no protocol-wide fund: a meme-coin market's blowup cannot touch the ETH market's backstop.

## Flows

**In:** maker + taker insurance [fees](fees.md) · seized liquidatee collateral · vault unwind profits · forfeited termination bonds · the listing seed (≥ $3,000)

**Out:** vault unwind losses (unload fills and ADL settlement alike) · keeper rewards (liquidation, vault unloading, price refresh, ADL execution) · UMA termination-proposer rewards

Seized collateral arrives **earmarked**. While the vault still holds the inventory it came in with, it is not capital the fund can spend and not value a share can redeem — it leaves again when that inventory closes, and only what the close did not need stays with LPs. Everything below that reads "free capital" means the balance net of that earmark.

Losses beyond the fund become `deficit` — realized bad debt — which [winds the pair down](termination.md) (the health check fixes the settlement price and opens the settlement window). The fund is the last cushion before winners get haircut: at finalize it becomes whatever remains after every trader entitlement is reserved, so bad debt, the deficit and the inventory's loss come out of it first, and only the shortfall beyond it reduces the winners' payout ratio.

## Target sizing

The fund's target is the pair's [stress move](margin.md#the-stress-move) — the same number maintenance margin is set to — clamped to **2%–10% of open interest**. The fund pays what a liquidation loses *beyond* maintenance margin, and for a fat-tailed return that shortfall scales with the quantile it is measured past, so the target is proportional to the level rather than reasoned about on a curve of its own. At the current 1× setting the fund is sized to absorb one further stress move beyond what traders already hold, on every pair alike: a calm market's 2% maintenance level asks for 2% coverage, a 100%-vol market's 4.5% asks for 4.5%, and a session pair's demonstrated 10% reopen asks for the full 10%. The clamps are the only place the target stops tracking margin — past the 10% ceiling a pair too risky to back is left to give on leverage instead, and the margin curve keeps climbing after the fund stops.

The taker insurance fee then steers the fund toward target: sharply higher when underfunded, and floored at its base rate — an overfull fund gets no discount, because new flow still consumes coverage and grows the very open interest the target is measured against. Excess is returned to LPs through the wider above-target withdrawal budget instead. Risk-*reducing* fills pay that base rate rather than the multiplied one — deleveraging is never punished. A depleted fund also tightens leverage, through the insurance multiplier on IMR, but it never moves the target: that would let a shortfall deepen its own measure.

A fifth of the target is a **reserve** (`ADL_RESERVE_BP`): the vault's [unloads](liquidations.md) never spend below it, the [ADL](adl.md) gate measures expected loss against the capital above it, and share pricing deducts an inventory loss only as far as that same capital covers it. The reserve is what stays for the realizations no bound can refuse — opposing liquidations netting at the oracle price, a seizure the deposits ledger could not back coming due — and for the keeper rewards the fund pays.

## LP mechanics

| Rule | Value |
|---|---|
| Minimum deposit | $5 |
| Share pricing | Shares are priced off the fund's **value at the mark**, not its balance. Both bases start from *free capital* — the fund less the collateral seized alongside inventory the vault still holds, which leaves again when that inventory unloads — and add the inventory's unrealized result. A **deposit** counts both legs, so a newcomer neither pays for a loss the incumbents underwrote nor buys a pending recovery at a discount. A **redemption** counts the loss the fund will bear and never an unrealized gain (it is not cash yet, and paying it would hand the leaver a price-timing option inside the 3-day window). A loss is deducted only as far as the fund's capital above the reserve covers it: beyond that the ADL closes the inventory at the estates' bankruptcy price and the winners take the rest, so the deduction stops where the fund's own exposure stops — and with the fund already at or below its reserve, nothing is deducted at all. With no inventory held the basis is free capital and a deposit reads no price. First deposit 1:1. A fund wiped below $1 by bad debt starts a new **share epoch** on the next deposit: pre-drain balances lazily read as zero (that stake truly went to zero) and the rescuer is minted 1:1 against only their own contribution. Fund value left with no live LPs is priced into permanently locked shares at `address(0)`, so a new depositor can't buy the pre-existing buffer for the price of their deposit. `BazaarPairLens.quoteInsuranceDeposit` / `quoteInsuranceWithdrawal` run the same arithmetic at a caller-supplied mark |
| Fresh-price requirement | A **redemption** on a live pair always needs a fresh mark — it prices the shares, judges vault health and sizes the rate limit — so the frozen closing mark of a non-continuously traded pair is refused outright; once the pair is finalized it needs no price at all, its inventory having been retired. A **deposit** needs one only while the vault holds inventory, the single case where it has to value something the frozen mark cannot see. The reason is the same on both sides: off-chain venues print the reopen hours before the oracle can, so shares priced against a frozen mark hand the difference to whoever can already see it — a redemption escaping a loss, or a deposit buying the inventory's recovery cheap — and there is no conservative figure to charge a depositor instead, because a reopen can print anywhere. Nothing is lost by waiting: a shut venue also freezes the machinery that capital would fund (ADL refuses a stale mark, liquidation refuses one older than 10 minutes), and the loss realizes at the reopen, where a fresh mark prices the flow correctly. With the vault flat a deposit reads no price, so recapitalizing a drained fund is never blocked by market hours |
| Withdrawal | two-step: request → **20-day cooldown** → **3-day execution window**. Requests are stamped with the share epoch: a fund recap voids pending requests, so a post-recap re-depositor starts a fresh cooldown |
| Rate limit (below target) | min(0.5% of OI notional, 5% of the fund) per 6-hour period — one cumulative budget shared by all LPs, so Sybil-splitting doesn't help; the fund term keeps each period's outflow a bounded fraction of what is left, since an OI-only budget would exceed a depleted fund outright |
| Rate limit (above target) | max(1% of OI, 10% of fund) against the same 6-hour budget |
| Voting maturity | shares must be 7 days old to propose or vote on insurer terminations, the listing seed included; deposit lots are tracked 21 days as snipe-vote defense |
| Deposits | ≤ 100 per user per rolling 7 days |

Withdrawals are blocked while the vault's liquidation exposure exceeds the ADL trigger or a termination is scheduled — LPs cannot front-run the exact events they are paid to backstop. The execute call judges vault health itself, against the price it fetches, rather than trusting a flag that only trading activity refreshes: on a quiet book that flag would sit stale while inherited inventory drifts into a loss the fund is about to absorb. The price it fetches must be fresh: on non-24/7 markets the frozen closing mark is refused, because off-chain venues print the reopen hours before the oracle can and the fund is sized to absorb exactly that gap — so LP exits happen during trading hours, and a market whose feed has died releases its LPs once the [stale-oracle termination](termination.md) settles it. They stay blocked while the [settlement window](termination.md) is open, because the fund's residual is only known at finalize; letting LPs exit first would push those losses onto traders. Shares committed to an active insurer-vote proposal are likewise locked until it resolves. Once a pair *is* finalized, the live gates open: LPs always have an exit after settlement, for whatever remains of the fund after bad debt, and the execute call needs no price update — a pair whose feed has died is exactly the case it must work in. One gate remains if finalize ran through the [escape hatch](termination.md) with positions still open: exits wait until those are settled — anyone, the LP included, can settle them for gas — or 14 days beyond the hatch at most, so a bankrupt straggler's shortfall is charged to the residual before any redemption is priced against it.

Insurance LPs also hold a governance right: proposing and voting on [insurer-vote termination](termination.md) of their pair — the people with capital at risk can shut the market down.
