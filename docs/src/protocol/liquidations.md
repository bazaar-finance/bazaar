# Liquidations

`liquidate(usersToLiquidate[], priceUpdate)` is permissionless — any keeper can liquidate any set of positions whose equity has fallen below maintenance margin (judged by the same [lagged-MMR solvency math](margin.md) the rest of the protocol uses, at plain oracle spot). The call recalculates the margin curve from that price before judging anyone, without storing it, and judges at that rate — capped by the stored rate inside the one-minute cooldown, the only time open interest opened earlier in the same transaction can have reached it. A requirement that has fallen since the last recompute — the fund topped up, open interest closed — therefore protects a position at once, even inside the cooldown, rather than when someone else refreshes. After the walk the call stores the curve through the normal cooldown, so the recorded rate reflects the seizures in the same transaction unless something stored it within the last minute, in which case it waits for the next refresh. Open interest opened earlier in the same transaction cannot reach the stored rate this way: the call that opened it refreshed first.

**How old a mark may confiscate collateral.** On a [stale fallback](oracle.md#the-stale-price-regime-market-hours) the call reverts once that stored price is more than **10 minutes** old (`MAX_LIQUIDATION_PRICE_AGE`). The fallback carries no expiry of its own — it stays authoritative until some call stores a newer one — and seizure takes a position's *entire* bucket rather than its shortfall, so without a ceiling an account that breached maintenance margin for a single tick would stay seizable at that tick for as long as the pair went untouched, long after the market traded back through it. The ceiling redirects liquidation onto a fresh price rather than withdrawing the power: past it, a caller supplying `priceUpdate` (and Pyth's fee in `msg.value`) liquidates normally.

**No restart grace on 24/7 pairs.** A continuously traded pair has no stale fallback, so a liquidation there always runs on a fresh price, and nothing holds it back after an outage. When the Arbitrum sequencer halts, or the pair's Pyth feed stops being pushed, the first transaction that brings a fresh price judges every account it names at the post-gap price. The protocol reads no sequencer-uptime signal and opens no grace window. Because seizure takes the whole bucket, an account that sits just under maintenance margin at the restart price loses all of its remaining equity, up to about MMR × notional, even when a small top-up would have cured it. The holder's defences race the keepers in the same block: `depositCollateral` needs no price and stays open, but nothing orders it ahead of a liquidation. A StopLoss set above the restart price gives no protection either. It is cancelled with the bucket if the account is seized first, and if that batch opens an [ADL](adl.md) window, trigger execution halts while liquidation carries on. The trade-off is deliberate: a grace would also delay the seizure of positions that are genuinely failing, and the fund would carry the gap through it. On a 24/7 pair, the only protection across an outage is margin held above maintenance. Session pairs differ: their gap risk is priced into margin, and their liquidations run on the stale-price rules above.

## What happens to a liquidated position

Liquidation is **full** — there are no partials:

1. The entire remaining collateral is seized into the pair's **insurance fund**.
2. The position itself transfers to the pair's **vault** at its *bankruptcy price* — the price at which its equity would have been exactly zero:

   ```
   long:  bankruptcy = (entryValue − collateral − fundingPnl) / size
   short: bankruptcy = (entryValue + collateral + fundingPnl) / size
   ```

3. The keeper earns, from the insurance fund and with no profit-contingent component, **$0.05** for each position the call seizes and each account it newly records as deferred — except that a seizure which [nets](#the-vault-one-aggregate-not-a-queue) against the vault's inventory earns the larger of $0.05 and **2 bp** of the notional the netting retires, valued at the liquidation price — and then whatever 2 bp of what the call's inline unload sells into the book exceeds the part of those rewards beyond the nettings' 2 bp. The per-account rewards are paid as soon as the call seizes or records the accounts, so they are due even when nothing can be sold, as while an ADL window is open. They are per account rather than per call so that batching is never penalized: one call naming ten accounts earns what ten single-account calls would, and costs the keeper less gas. The rate is paid on exposure the call actually took off the fund, not on exposure it moved onto the fund: a netting retires its overlap with the inventory at once, while a seizure that joins the inventory, or the part of a netted position larger than the inventory, is paid for when it is retired — at 2 bp by the call's own unload, a later `unloadVault` or a later netting, or through the averted-loss reward of an [ADL](adl.md) batch. A call that seizes nothing and records nothing new earns no flat reward (one that names an already-listed account still runs the inline unload and earns 2 bp of whatever it sells), and an account named twice in one call is paid for once, so naming solvent, already-listed or repeated accounts cannot farm the $0.05. The one exception is an account the call first defers and then, named again after a seizure that made its netting affordable, seizes: that pays twice, exactly as the deferring call and a later seizing call would. An account whose position is worth less than **$5** (the order minimum) at the liquidation price counts for at most its own equity rather than the full $0.05. The order minimum bounds orders, not positions: a reduce sized just under the position, or a small partial fill, can leave a position that small sitting at or near zero equity without any price move, so an unconditional $0.05 would pay the fund out once per such account. A position of $5 or more is paid the full $0.05 even past bankruptcy. It holds at least $0.10 of equity at the maintenance line and only a real price move carries it further, and until it is seized its further losses are the fund's. (The fund is debited and the keeper's claimable balance credited in the same step — no transfer runs inside the liquidation, so nothing about the keeper's address can block it. The reward is capped at the fund's *free capital*: the collateral just seized is earmarked against the inventory it arrived with and never pays the keeper, so a fund holding nothing else pays nothing, and the liquidations themselves still commit.)
4. The user's TP/SL orders are canceled and the bucket fully reset. Resting limit and stop-limit orders are not canceled by the liquidation.

## The vault: one aggregate, not a queue

Inherited inventory is aggregated into a single net, single-direction holding — sums of size, entry notional, bankruptcy notional, and a size-weighted entry funding index. There is no per-estate FIFO: unwinding is O(1) regardless of how many accounts were liquidated.

A liquidation on the *opposite* side nets against the aggregate immediately when the fund can afford it: the overlapping quantity's price legs cancel at oracle and funding legs cancel at the current index, realizing PnL on the spot; the remainder can flip the aggregate's direction. The netting's loss is the two estates' entry gap on the overlap, price and funding legs together, which does not depend on the market price. It runs only when that loss fits in the two slices' seizure plus whatever of the fund's free capital **above the reserve** and the ledger's arrears exceeds what the inventory that survives the netting needs to close at its own bankruptcy price beyond its earmark: the collateral the ledger never paid at seizure plus the funding accrued while the vault held it. The arrears are one pair-wide balance, and spent on a netting they would be missing when the survivors settle, where the shortfall would surface as `deficit` with no insolvency verdict in between. For a `liquidate()` the survivors are the whole remainder; an [ADL](adl.md) batch that has closed what netting every listed account needs keeps backed only what those nettings leave. The loss is paid from the collateral seized with the two netted slices first, and only then net of the arrears and from the fund's free capital — never from the earmark of inventory that survives the netting. Netting the arrears first would spend the survivors' share on a loss the slices' own seizure covers and leave that seizure in free capital, which, while the fund sits below its reserve, neither this gate nor the ADL gate counts: the survivors would read short although the fund held the same value, their next netting could be refused, and their loss at the mark could open a window. Paid from the seizure first, a netting reaches the arrears only by what its loss exceeds it, within what the gate found spendable. The cap on what the netting may spend is kept as the backstop, and anything it cannot cover is realized as `deficit` in the same transaction.

When the netting would breach the reserve the position is **deferred** instead: left open, recorded (at most `MAX_DEFERRED_LIQUIDATIONS` = 16 at once; when full, a newcomer replaces the listed account with the smallest loss beyond its collateral, if its own loss is larger), and re-evaluated by every later `liquidate()` that names it, which seizes it as soon as the netting fits or the inventory it would have netted against is gone, and by every [ADL](adl.md) batch, whose inventory leg closes enough inventory to make the netting fit when no cohort covers the account and it is past its collateral — at the mark wherever the inventory's own exposure does not need the close — or, when no close short of the whole inventory would, retires the inventory so the account becomes the new one. The list lasts only as long as a window is wanted for it: any health check that leaves no window open — resolving one, or finding none to open, as after the call's own inline unload has sold the inventory the account was deferred against — empties the list, because no window means no listed account still carries loss beyond its collateral while the fund covers the inventory, or there is no inventory left to net one against, so each is an ordinary liquidation candidate again. A deferral on the stale-price fallback keeps its entry until a fresh price opens its window, since a stale mark opens none. Its loss beyond its collateral holds an [ADL](adl.md) window open, and the auction closes its own counterparties against it at its bankruptcy price — the loss lands on the winners it belongs to rather than on the fund. A liquidator earns the same flat reward for each account it newly records as deferred as for each one it seizes, capped the same way below $5; re-submitting a listed account, or one that is skipped because the list is full, pays nothing.

## Exiting the inventory

The vault is not a market maker — it exits as fast as the book allows:

- **Inline, inside `liquidate()` itself**: freshly seized inventory is crossed into the resting book before the transaction re-evaluates vault health, within a fixed node budget (`LIQ_INLINE_UNLOAD_STEPS` = 16) that keeps the liquidator's gas predictable. At that instant only bids inside the band fill; bids beyond it wait for the floor described below.
- **Permissionless `unloadVault(maxSteps, priceUpdate)`**: any keeper finishes deeper inventory for **2 bp of the notional it unloads**, from the insurance fund. The rate is paid on any amount, so a keeper that drains a thin book is never left with nothing; a **$0.25** floor (`UNLOAD_BOUNTY_MIN`) applies only when at least **$1,250** unloads (where the rate overtakes it) or the call clears the inventory, which is what stops a keeper earning a premium by slicing one unload into many. Capped at the fund's free capital — never paid from the seizure earmarked against inventory still held.
- **Opposite-side netting** (above).
- **[Auto-deleveraging](adl.md)** when the book can't absorb it and the fund is threatened.

Both unload paths fill only inside the **unload bound** and within the fund's **headroom**. The bound is built from two anchors: the estates' *fund-neutral price* — the price at which closing the inventory pays back exactly what backs it in the fund's hands, net of the funding accrued since the estates entered (their entry basis less that backing, carried forward by that funding) — and the **band edge, min(5%, MMR) from oracle** (`LIQ_MAX_SLIPPAGE_BP`), where the MMR is the lower of the current rate and its lowest hourly sample over the past day — the current rate follows live open interest, which a matched pair of accounts can inflate for a minute, and a wider band would move its edge toward bankruptcy, where fills are immediate. The backing is the collateral seized with the inventory *plus the ledger's arrears* (below), which settlement nets off the fund's payment, so with both counted the neutral price is the estates' own bankruptcy price however much of their collateral the ledger could actually hand over — counting the seizure alone would read an estate as bankrupt by the amount its winners had already withdrawn and refuse fills the fund loses nothing on. The arrears are one pair-wide balance, though, and an inventory that closes without a loss large enough to consume the arrears it created leaves them behind, so the neutral price is held from crossing the estates' bankruptcy price: the vault never sells inventory below (or buys it back above) the price at which the estates' own collateral ran out, whatever else the ledger owes the fund. The hold also forgoes funding the inventory has received since seizure, which the frozen bankruptcy figure does not carry. A bid inside the band fills at once, including one under bankruptcy. A bid beyond the band that still beats bankruptcy fills only once the vault's **floor** has opened to it: the floor starts at the band edge when the inventory is seized and slides linearly to the neutral price over **3 minutes** (`UNLOAD_FLOOR_DECAY`), measured from the inventory's size-weighted seizure time (a large estate joining later restarts most of the countdown, a dust one barely moves it, and inventory leaving does not touch it). The region between the band edge and bankruptcy is the estates' residual equity; offering it to the whole market for a few minutes means its price is set by competing bids rather than by whoever bids in the seizing transaction — the liquidator included, who could otherwise rest a bid a hair above bankruptcy and sell the vault its own seizure in the same call. A bid under both anchors is refused. The headroom is what the fund may spend on fills inside that bound: its free capital above a **reserve** of 10% of the [insurance target](insurance.md) on open interest (`ADL_RESERVE_BP`; the lower of live open interest and its lowest hourly reading over the past day), less the loss the inventory already carries at the mark, less the caller's reward. A fill is measured against a reference price: beyond it a fill lowers the loss the remainder carries by at least what it costs and is always taken; inside it a fill costs the difference per unit, and the walk takes only what the headroom covers, trimming the last maker and stopping. While the fund's capital above the reserve covers the inventory's loss at the mark — the healthy side of the ADL gate — the reference is the nearer of the neutral price and the oracle, because the headroom has already set that loss aside: spending it to zero leaves the fund above its reserve by exactly the remaining inventory's loss at the mark, so the pair ends the walk healthy. An unload's fills can never take the fund below its reserve, and never push a pair over the ADL trigger — which is why health is judged *after* the walk, letting stale bids absorb inventory before any auction opens. The caller's reward, paid after the walk, is held to the same line: fills at or beyond the reference are taken whatever the headroom, so where the inventory left carries a loss at the mark the reward takes no more than what the fund's capital above the reserve covers that loss by, and cannot open the auction either; where the inventory carries no loss at the mark the reserve may pay it, and a walk that started past the trigger pays its reward from free capital, the reserve included, since the auction opens regardless. A walk that starts past the trigger has nothing set aside, so its reference is the neutral price itself: it takes only fills that cost the fund nothing, spends neither the base nor the reserve, cannot book a deficit, and leaves the rest to the auction. Neither path fills against a stale oracle. Off-chain venues print the reopen before the oracle can, so a maker who can see it would take the inventory at the frozen mark only when the reopen favours the fund, which would then exit exactly when holding pays and hold exactly when it loses. A liquidation on the stale fallback still seizes, inside its 10-minute age limit (see [Oracle](oracle.md#the-stale-price-regime-market-hours)), but its inline unload waits, and `unloadVault` fills nothing and pays nothing until the first fresh print.

Vault PnL from these exits is booked as an insurance↔deposits transfer (profits refill the fund; losses drain it, never past the headroom). Until an exit books it, the loss has not reached the deposits ledger: the seizure moved the estate's whole collateral into the fund, including the part that covers the counterparties' gains, so the ledger sits below the claims on it by the inventory's loss at the mark, and a realized winner's withdrawal beyond what the ledger holds reverts with `CollateralLib__LedgerShortfall(amount, available)`. The cash is in the fund, earmarked against the inventory, and any exit — an unload, an opposing netting, an ADL close — pays it back; a winner still holding the position can rest an order inside the bound and call `unloadVault` to be that exit, and `available` is withdrawable meanwhile. The stuck tail is bounded by the inventory's loss less the margin the winning side's new holders posted, which is nothing after a prompt liquidation at the maintenance line and something only after a gap or a late seizure. `deficit` — realized bad debt that [winds the pair down](termination.md) — is reserved for genuine insolvency: a loss the fund cannot cover once the estate settles. A seizure the deposits ledger could not pay in full (winners had already realized against the position and withdrawn) is carried as the ledger's **arrears** to the fund, netted off the fund's payment when the estate settles and subtracted from what traders are owed at finalize, so the fund's outlay on an estate is never more than the loss beyond its bankruptcy price. Every exit — a netting, an unload fill, an [ADL](adl.md) close — pays its loss first out of the collateral seized with the units it settles, then net of the arrears, and only then from free capital. An exit that loses no more than that collateral and the units' share of the arrears therefore leaves the inventory behind it at least as backed per unit as before, and one that loses more draws on the arrears and free capital by exactly the excess. Netted from the arrears first, a fill the walk takes as free would leave the inventory reading worse per unit while the released collateral sat in free capital below the reserve, where the ADL gate does not count it, and the walk could open the auction it is there to avoid. Arrears no open inventory relies on stay a receivable until a later loss nets them or finalize collects them. Note that once a pair's settlement price is fixed, this live liquidation path is off: positions are marked to the frozen price by permissionless settlement instead.

Every liquidation fill also feeds two EMAs, split at the oracle, and they go to different places. **Slippage** is the fill against the oracle — the execution cost of closing the inventory — and adds straight into the pair's [stress move](margin.md#the-stress-move) at full size, because maintenance margin has to pay for getting out. **Drift** is the oracle against the estates' average bankruptcy price — how far the market ran past the level margin was set to survive while the vault held the position — and feeds the [insurance target](insurance.md), because that overrun is precisely the loss the fund exists to absorb. Measuring both against the bankruptcy price alone fuses them into one number that reads as neither: blind to a thin book whenever slippage stays inside MMR, and unbounded above it, since a short bought back in a squeeze can print arbitrarily far past bankruptcy. A reading from one episode halves three days after it and keeps falling harmonically, so a market that stops liquidating badly recedes to its volatility curve on its own. Each reading keeps its own anchor, which a later batch moves toward the present only by its share of that reading (evidence merged into an older reading takes on part of its age), and each is stored to a fraction of a basis point, so a stream of dust unloads can neither restart the fade nor round a reading away. The clock advances only as prices are stored and, on a session pair, skips any stretch of 12 h or more without one (see [Margin](margin.md#the-stress-move)), since a shut venue teaches nothing and a reading taken during the silence must not fade through a closure that has not ended yet. Unloads fill only against a fresh oracle, so neither leg ever records oracle error as book depth. Each fill is recorded floored at zero before it is weighted: a fill better than the oracle, or an oracle still short of bankruptcy, is value the unload has already paid into the fund, so it counts as a zero-cost sample at full weight rather than as credit against the next real cost. Slippage is also capped at `LIQ_MAX_SLIPPAGE_BP`, the unload bound's own band — past it a fill only lands when the market sits that far past bankruptcy in the fund's favor, where the walk takes any maker that still beats it. Drift is never capped: a large reading is a genuine overrun. Each batch is weighed against the evidence already accumulated or 1% of per-side open interest (`LIQ_GAP_PRIOR_FLOOR_BP`), whichever is larger, so it moves both readings by its share of the book: a dust unload by a fraction of a basis point, and no single estate — including one its own liquidator buys back from the vault — can replace the pair's history (see [Margin](margin.md#the-stress-move)).
