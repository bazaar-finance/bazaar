# Termination & Settlement

Perpetuals on real-world assets need an exit ramp: feeds get decommissioned, companies get acquired, assets redenominate. Every Bazaar market can be wound down **without anyone's permission** — four independent paths, each ending with positions cash-settled at a defined price and withdrawals open forever after.

## The four paths

| Path | Trigger | Gate |
|---|---|---|
| **Scheduled** (UMA) | A cessation event, already occurred or officially scheduled with a fixed date (an announced-but-unapproved merger or a contemplated split is not grounds): delisting, oracle decommission, structural change (splits, spinoffs, special dividends > 5%, redenominations), or Pyth contract migration | $1,000 bond, 12 h UMA liveness; the proposal declares one of the four categories as a number the contract renders into the claim; `lastTradingTs` must be ≥ 2 h in the future (the insurer vote's minimum lead); acceptance alone enforces it as proposed only when it was ≥ 12 h (the liveness) out, so an undisputed claim is accepted before its cutoff and the print there is still unknowable — an event that has already happened is proposed at that earliest enforceable cutoff — while a nearer cutoff is met as proposed only through the insurer vote every claim opens on its own cutoff; without the vote, the claim is accepted after its cutoff, which then moves to 7 s after acceptance, as for any late acceptance. Acceptance is final: nothing can withdraw, delay or cancel it. A later UMA proposal with an earlier cutoff or an insurer vote can bring it forward, and the stale-oracle and autonomous paths end the pair at once. Each acceptance can only move the cutoff earlier, so two proposals settled while both cutoffs are still ahead resolve to the earlier one whichever settles first; one settled after its own cutoff lands 7 s after the settle, as a first acceptance would, and only if that is still earlier |
| **Stale oracle** | No Pyth update for **21 days** — objectively provable on-chain, no UMA needed | none; anyone |
| **Insurer vote** | Matured insurance shares voting yes reach **60%** of the share snapshot, no later than one hour before the cutoff. Every UMA termination claim opens a vote on its own cutoff; a bare proposal names a cutoff with no claim behind it | Claim-backed: the claim's UMA bond, nothing more; bare: $1,000 bond (forfeited to the fund if the vote fails) and a mature stake to file. 4-day share maturity at the opening of the vote window, the listing seed included; a bare cutoff sits **2 h to 14 days** after the proposal, a claim's vote opens 14 days before its cutoff, so every vote has at least an hour; any number of proposals per pair. A vote-scheduled pair settles under the verdict on its claim; a bare vote or a rejected claim costs the yes-voters a 13-day lock and a levy ([below](#settlement-price-discipline)) |
| **Autonomous** | Realized bad debt (`deficit > 0`), ADL timeout (24 h, plus a 30 min fresh-priced runway — see [ADL](adl.md)), or books-vs-balance shortfall | none — self-executing inside the health check |

The autonomous path fixes the settlement price at the live price mid-transaction and opens the same settlement window as every other path, rather than terminating on the spot. Freezing withdrawals instantly is precisely what the insolvency check needs, and routing through the window means those positions get the same per-user settlement as a planned wind-down. A books-vs-balance shortfall takes the same route: the missing cash simply shows up in the payout ratios at finalize.

Market-driven price moves are explicitly *not* valid termination grounds — anticipated volatility is tradeable risk, not a structural event.

## Settlement price discipline

Acceptance freezes the pair's price feed at the cutoff — `lastTradingTs`, or 7 s after acceptance when `lastTradingTs` is sooner than that — and the pair then settles at the **first Pyth tick published in `[ts − 3 s, ts]`** (`MAX_PRICE_STALENESS`, the same bound every live price is held to) — a signed historical update, verified on-chain, never a number typed into the proposal. The update carries its predecessor's publish time and Pyth checks it against the window's start, so the tick is provably the window's first print: whoever settles cannot choose among the prints inside the window, a later one is refused like one outside it, and for a composite each leg is pinned on its own (see [Supplying the settlement prints](#supplying-the-settlement-prints)). For the first 12 hours after the cutoff only that genuine tick is accepted (bad or empty data cannot force a fallback while a real tick may exist); after the grace, it falls back to the frozen last stored price so a market can never strand un-terminated. The grace matches the proposal liveness because it answers the same question — how long an interested party needs to notice a termination and act — and it has to carry that alone: posting the tick costs gas plus the Pyth fee and pays nothing, while waiting the window out and calling with empty calldata costs only gas, so whichever side the stale mark favors has the cheaper action. It is not longer because limbo freezes withdrawals, matching and liquidation until the price is fixed; posting the tick ends limbo immediately, so the length only ever delays the fallback. Trading, matching, liquidation, deposits and withdrawals halt **6 s before** the scheduled timestamp (`LIMBO_LEAD`, two windows), so the last block that trades is stamped a window and a second before the window opens. A fill executes at the maker's price whatever print its transaction carries, so a trader who has seen the settlement print can pick off every quote on the wrong side of it; with one window of lead that block would sit a second before the window, and a Pyth clock running ahead of the chain's by more than a print's delivery time would put the print within reach. At two windows the skew must pass a full window on top of that delivery time, and the lead stays that short because no position can be liquidated during it; once the price is fixed, the live liquidation engine is off entirely and positions change only through settlement at that frozen price.

Binding the price to a signed Pyth tick stops a proposer *inventing* a number, and requiring a cutoff that acceptance alone enforces to sit at least the liveness in the future stops them *choosing* one: the price at `lastTradingTs` is unknown when the proposal is filed, so no historical print can be shopped. A cutoff nearer than that, down to the insurer vote's two-hour lead, is enforced as proposed only by the vote, whose latch closes an hour before the cutoff with the print still unknowable; if nobody votes it through, the claim is accepted after its cutoff and moved like any late acceptance, below. Requiring the window's first print does the same for whoever settles, who would otherwise pick among every print in the 3 s window with all of them already public. A proposal accepted after its cutoff — a disputed one resolving at the DVM days later, an expired one nobody settled at its cutoff, or one whose cutoff was nearer than the liveness and that no vote carried — is scheduled 7 s after acceptance instead (the limbo lead plus a second, so limbo starts one second after the acceptance block and the window opens a window and a second after it, the same margin every last trading block keeps): nothing halts the market before acceptance, so the book has traded at live prices past the proposed cutoff while the tick at that cutoff was public, and settling there would price every position opened since against a known answer. A cutoff less than 7 s away at acceptance is moved out the same way. An event that has already happened — a feed that went dark, an asset already delisted — is proposed at the earliest cutoff the contract accepts; if the feed has no tick there, the pair settles once the grace elapses at its last stored price, the last fresh, confidence-checked print the pair itself recorded. A feed dead for more than 21 days can also be terminated through the stale-oracle path without any proposal.

An insurer-vote termination is scheduled, not fixed. Every UMA termination claim opens a vote on its own cutoff the moment it is filed, and an insurer with a mature stake may also file a bare proposal that names a cutoff with no claim behind it. A bare cutoff sits at least two hours out, which leaves even the shortest vote an hour before it closes; a claim's vote opens fourteen days before its cutoff, or at filing when that is nearer, and its snapshot is taken at the first touch once the window is open — the filing itself, or else the first vote — so a claim filed months ahead is counted against the supply of the fortnight before its cutoff. The moment the vote reaches 60% of the snapshot — anyone may execute it, in the same block the votes land if a single holder has them — the pair is scheduled to stop trading at that cutoff, or its existing schedule is pulled earlier to it. Voting and the latch both close **one hour before the cutoff**: the print at the cutoff is never in sight when the pair is scheduled, so a coalition cannot file staggered proposals and decide at each cutoff, with the price known, whether to settle. Proposals are independent — any number per pair, the earliest scheduled cutoff wins, and a verified UMA cutoff earlier than a pending insurer schedule pulls it in — so a proposal that never reaches the threshold blocks nothing, and the only way to make a pair settle later than the honest majority wants is to be that majority. Votes are never withdrawn: a voted share stays locked against insurance withdrawal until its proposal's cutoff or the pair's, whichever comes first, and a failed proposal releases it sooner only while the pair carries no cutoff. The same shares may back several proposals at once, at most eight live ones per voter on a pair.

What a vote-scheduled pair settles at depends on the verdict on the claim behind it, stamped when the price is fixed. A claim UMA upholds settles under UMA's own rule at the vote's cutoff — the first print in the 3 s window before it, or the stored price once 12 hours pass without one — because every category UMA upholds is a break in the feed's continuity after which the next print is no price at all. Those 12 hours count from the cutoff or, if it is later, from when the verdict became known: the end of the claim's 12-hour liveness if nobody disputed it, or the moment its result is recorded on-chain if someone did — by `settleTerminationProposal`, by the settlement call itself, or by anyone settling the assertion on UMA directly. Until then the settlement waits on the verdict and nobody can post the print, so a grace counted from the cutoff alone could run out before anyone had the chance, leaving whoever settles first after a late verdict to choose between the print and the stored price. A disputed verdict recorded by a settlement call that then fails is rolled back with it, so the stored price is reachable only 12 hours after a recording that stands. Nothing is costed: the lock on the yes-voters lifts with the verdict. A claim UMA rejects settles under the **chain rule** — the median of five Pyth prints chained from the cutoff, described next — its yes-voters stay locked for the 13-day withdrawal cooldown after the fix, the cooldown a redemption on the live pair would have served, and they forfeit half of every share they voted. A bare vote, or a claim with no verdict 14 days after it was filed, settles and locks the same way and forfeits half of the dissent fraction of the voted shares — the share of the snapshot that did not vote yes — and never less than a tenth of them, so a bare 60% pays a fifth and a unanimous shutdown, a holder of the whole fund included, still pays a tenth: ending a market with no judge has a base price that only a claim UMA upholds escapes. The forfeited shares are credited at redemption to the bug bounty address, the same address that takes the [bug bounty tax](fees.md) on trading fees; it redeems them like any other holder, so the price every other holder redeems at is untouched whatever order they leave in, and no voter or abstainer can recapture a forfeit. An unjudged vote whose schedule met a cutoff UMA verified — one it pulled in, or one accepted after it — no more than the fourteen days a vote may act in after its own, is not costed: UMA confirmed the event it moved the halt ahead of. A verified cutoff months out vouches for nothing about a halt tomorrow, so a vote that pulls a far schedule in is costed like any other. While the verdict is pending the fix waits, holding the pair in limbo past its cutoff: settling first at the pre-event price and penalizing the voters later could never make whole the traders that print shortchanged if the claim proved false. A claim rejected before its vote has latched ends the vote, and its votes are released as a failed proposal's are.

**The chain rule.** A vote-scheduled pair whose claim UMA has not upheld — a rejected claim, a claim with no verdict in time, or a bare vote — settles at the **median of five Pyth prints chained from the cutoff**: the first is the first print published at or after the cutoff, and each later one is the first print published at least **10 minutes** after the one before it (`SETTLEMENT_SAMPLE_COUNT`, `SETTLEMENT_SAMPLE_SPACING`). Every sample is proven the way a single settlement tick is: its signed update carries its predecessor's publish time, and Pyth checks that against the sample's own target, which the contract computes from the publish time it has just verified for the sample before. The five prints are therefore fixed by the feed's history, and whoever settles cannot choose which prints count — other prints, the same prints in another order, or fewer than five are all refused. The proposer chose the cutoff, so a single print there would be the cheapest price in the market to lean on; the median costs at least three prints to move. Measuring each target from the previous sample, rather than from the cutoff, keeps the samples apart when the feed goes quiet: a cutoff at a session's close yields the closing print and then prints from the reopen onward, so the median is a price from the reopened session rather than the close, and never the opening print repeated five times. For a composite pair each sample is one print per leg, composed, and the next target is measured from the later of the two legs. Five samples ten minutes apart span at least 40 minutes of printing, so the pair cannot settle sooner than that after the cutoff.

The chain rule has no grace, and no stored-price fallback until its last resort opens: 21 days after the cutoff, or 12 hours after a capping UMA cutoff (below) if that is sooner. A cutoff that falls in a closed session therefore settles on prints from the reopen, so the fund cannot pick a cutoff after the close and settle at a pre-news price unless UMA has judged the event real. Once the last resort opens, a call that brings no valid chain — none at all included — settles at the stored price, so whoever calls first then decides between a chain and the stored price; by then anyone who wanted the chain has had that long to post it, and without the fallback an expired guardian set, an archival gap, or a feed that went dark before printing five times would freeze the pair for good. An insurer cutoff that meets a termination UMA has accepted — a vote that pulls the UMA schedule earlier, or a UMA acceptance that lands after the insurer schedule and so leaves the earlier insurer cutoff in place — still halts trading first, but every sample of its chain must be published **no later than UMA's cutoff** (`umaCutoffCap`). The cap is the cutoff UMA verified, whenever the claim is settled: a claim settled after its own cutoff still caps at that cutoff, not at the moment it was settled, so when someone chooses to settle it cannot decide whether a chain's later samples count. A claim whose cutoff precedes the insurer cutoff caps nothing if it resolves after the pair has halted: the pair traded through that event before it stopped, so no price on the near side of it is left to keep. The cap binds only once the claim is accepted: a chain that fixes the price before then is final. A later UMA acceptance can only tighten the bound, since its cutoff must beat the one in force, and one earlier than the insurer cutoff that resolves before the pair halts pulls the schedule in under UMA's rule. If no such chain exists — including when the feed prints for less than 40 minutes between the two cutoffs — the pair settles at its stored price once UMA's cutoff is 12 hours old, or once the insurer cutoff is 21 days old if that comes sooner. UMA has verified an event the market can see coming, so without the cap a coalition could vote a cutoff into the closed session ahead of it and settle every position at post-event reopen prints instead of the pre-event price UMA's own rule falls back to. When the claim upheld is the one behind the vote itself, its cutoff becomes the UMA cutoff in force, which a later claim must beat. A proposal belongs to the share generation its snapshot was taken in: if the fund is drained and recapitalized while it is open, its votes and snapshot count shares that no longer exist, so it is voided, a bare bond refunded, and its votes lock nothing. A cutoff it already scheduled stands. From 6 s before the cutoff the pair is frozen for settlement: matching, orders, liquidation, deposits, withdrawals and ADL all wait, and the price refresh no longer runs the health check, so nothing can settle the book at the frozen pre-cutoff mark instead.

A stale-oracle termination settles at the pair's own last stored price — the oracle is read only as a liveness probe; the 21-day gate is enforced against the stored price's age, and against the probe's publish time as well whenever the oracle still returns a confidence-cleared tick. It is refused once a vote-set cutoff has passed: that pair settles through `terminateScheduledPair`, whose own last resort reaches the same stored price with the vote's outcome stamped.

A pair that never stored a price — its feed died before any trade or refresh recorded one — settles at a nominal price of 1 (the smallest the contract accepts) on every path that would otherwise fall back to the stored price, and the stale-oracle path ages it from its creation instead. Every price a pair resolves is one it stores or the one it already holds, so such a pair has only ever been marked at zero and the nominal price values its book exactly as its own mark did. Without it there would be no exit at all: a live pair pays insurers only against a fresh price, so the fund, the listing seed included, would stay locked for good. Aging from creation keeps the same path from ending a new listing whose feed's cached tick was already old when it listed.

The proposer of a UMA termination whose acceptance schedules the pair earns 0.1% of the pair's insurance fund (capped at $100 and at the fund's free capital), credited to their claimable balance on the pair and withdrawn with `claimFees()` — a bounty for noticing dead markets. It is paid once per pair, to the first acceptance that schedules it: a later one that brings the cutoff forward earns nothing, so re-proposing an event a second earlier pays no one. An acceptance that only caps an earlier insurer cutoff pays nothing: the insurers had already ended the market. A cutoff the insurers latched before the claim behind it resolved pays nothing either: the vote scheduled the pair, not the acceptance. The bond is quoted from `requiredTerminationBond()` rather than the $1,000 constant: UMA derives its own minimum from an owner-settable final fee, so the constant is a floor that tracks upward.

### Supplying the settlement prints

`terminateScheduledPair(pair, priceUpdate)` is permissionless and needs signed Pyth updates as calldata, fetched from Pyth's historical price API. Pyth's on-chain cache holds only the latest print, so it can neither serve a past print nor prove one was the first; every settlement print is therefore submitted as an archived update and verified on the spot. A print counts as the **first at or after** a timestamp when its `prevPublishTime` (the publish time of the feed's previous print, signed inside the update) is earlier than that timestamp. That is the query Pyth's historical API answers directly: the first update published at or after a given time, for a given feed. Submit the updates with the Pyth fee in ETH; any unused fee is refunded.

`settlementRule(pair)` on the terminator says which rule applies and what to fetch. It returns `(rule, firstTarget, latestPublishTs, lastResortTs, verdictDeadline)`:

| `rule` | Meaning | Submit |
|---|---|---|
| `SETTLE_WINDOW` (1) | UMA's rule: a UMA-scheduled pair, or a vote whose claim UMA upheld | one update per leg: the first print at or after `firstTarget` (the cutoff − 3 s), published no later than `latestPublishTs` (the cutoff). Fee: `BazaarOracle.getUpdateFee(updates)` |
| `SETTLE_CHAIN` (2) | the chain rule: a bare vote, a rejected claim, or a claim with no verdict 14 days after filing | five samples chained from `firstTarget` (the cutoff), every one published no later than `latestPublishTs` — see the steps below. Fee: `BazaarOracle.getSampleSetFee(baseFeedId, updates)` |
| `SETTLE_AWAIT_VERDICT` (3) | the vote that set the cutoff waits for UMA's verdict on its claim | nothing yet. Once UMA can resolve the claim, `settleTerminationProposal(id)` records the verdict, and `terminateScheduledPair` records it itself: an undisputed claim past its liveness, or a disputed one the DVM has ruled on. If no verdict has been recorded by `verdictDeadline`, the view reports `SETTLE_CHAIN`, but a DVM ruling the call records still decides the rule (see step 1 below) |
| `SETTLE_NOTHING` (0) | no cutoff is scheduled, or the price is already fixed | nothing |

Under `SETTLE_WINDOW` and `SETTLE_CHAIN`, once `lastResortTs` has passed a call with no updates at all settles the pair at its stored price: 12 hours after the cutoff under UMA's rule, or after the verdict became known for a vote whose claim UMA upheld, if that is later; 21 days after it under the chain rule, or 12 hours after a UMA cutoff the schedule met if that is sooner. Until then only the proven prints settle the pair, so empty or bad data cannot force the fallback while real prints may exist.

**Ending a market under the chain rule, step by step.**

1. **Wait for the cutoff, then record any verdict.** `pair.scheduledTerminationTs()` is the cutoff `T`; nothing settles until a block after it. If a claim-backed vote set the cutoff (`insurerScheduleId(pair)` is its assertion id), call `settleTerminationProposal(id)` first: the view cannot see a verdict the DVM has reached until someone records it, and `terminateScheduledPair` would apply that verdict instead. A revert there just means UMA cannot resolve the claim yet. If the claim was disputed and the DVM upheld it, recording the verdict starts its 12-hour grace, so have the window's print ready first, or submit that print to `terminateScheduledPair` directly, which records the verdict and settles in one call. Then check `settlementRule(pair)` returns `SETTLE_CHAIN` (2), and note `firstTarget` (= `T`) and `latestPublishTs`. If it returns `SETTLE_WINDOW` (1) instead, UMA upheld the claim: submit the window's print as the table above describes, before `lastResortTs`.
2. **Fetch the first sample.** Ask Pyth's historical API for the first update published at or after `T`, once per leg of the pair's feed (a composite pair has two legs: `BazaarOracle.composites(baseFeedId)` lists them). Read each update's publish time from the response.
3. **Chain the next four.** Set the next target to the sample's publish time plus 600 seconds — for a composite, the *later* of the two legs' publish times — and fetch the first update at or after it, per leg. Repeat until you hold five samples. The targets cannot be computed in advance: each depends on when the previous sample was actually published, so the requests run in sequence, and the last succeeds only once its print exists, at least 40 minutes after `T`.
4. **Check the bound.** Every sample's publish time must be no later than `latestPublishTs`. If one is not, no chain exists under the bound; go to step 6.
5. **Submit.** Order the updates sample by sample — one per sample for a direct feed, two per sample for a composite, the two legs of a sample in either order. Read the fee with `BazaarOracle.getSampleSetFee(baseFeedId, updates)` and call `terminateScheduledPair{value: fee}(pair, updates)`. The call verifies all five samples, fixes the settlement price at their median, and opens the [settlement window](#the-settlement-window).
6. **If no chain can be built** — the feed died before printing five times, the archive cannot serve a sample, or a UMA cutoff leaves too little room — wait until `lastResortTs`, then call `terminateScheduledPair(pair, [])` with no updates: the pair settles at its stored price.

For example, take an equity pair whose cutoff is Friday 16:00:00, the close. The first sample is the closing print at 16:00:00. The second target is 16:10:00; the feed's first print at or after it is Monday's open at 09:30:00. The remaining targets are 09:40:00, 09:50:00 and 10:00:00, and the set can be submitted from just after 10:00:00 on Monday. Its median is a price from Monday's first half hour, not the Friday close and not the opening print alone.

What a refused call means:

- **`PriceFeedNotFoundWithinRange`** (from Pyth): a sample is not the first print at or after its target — a later print, a print from the wrong slot, the samples out of order — or it was published after `latestPublishTs`. Under UMA's rule, the window's print is missing or not the first. Re-derive each target from the previous sample's verified publish time as in step 3.
- **`BazaarOracle__SampleCountMismatch(updates, expected)`**: the set does not hold one update per leg for each of five samples. No updates at all produces it too, until the last resort opens.
- **`BazaarPairTerminator__InsurerVerdictPending(deadline)`**: the claim behind the vote has no verdict yet; see `SETTLE_AWAIT_VERDICT`.
- **`BazaarPairTerminator__InsufficientPythFee(provided, required)`**: under the chain rule, the ETH sent is below `getSampleSetFee`; send at least that. Under UMA's rule an underfunded call fails inside the oracle call with no named error — and once `lastResortTs` has passed, that failure settles at the stored price — so send at least `getUpdateFee`.

Two further rules apply to every settlement print:

- **Composite pairs need one update per leg** for each print, each that leg's own first print. A UMA-rule print is one sample; a chain is five.
- **No confidence cap applies.** Each print has exactly one candidate, so a cap could only refuse it. A wide print settles as it is, and only a non-positive price is rejected.

## Proposer-supplied text

The UMA proposal takes one free-text argument — `proposeTermination(pair, category, lastTradingTs, reason)` — and it ends up inside the claim UMA voters read. The claim names the pair itself, by its pair ID and Pyth feed IDs, and tells voters to identify the asset from those feeds, so the market's identity in the claim is never the proposer's word. Because the claim is the entire basis on which a human decides to dispute, the reason is treated as hostile input:

- **Length**: `reason` is 1 to 1,000 bytes.
- **Charset**: letters, digits, space, and `. , & / -`, plus `: ? = # % _ ~ +` so evidence URLs survive. Every other byte — including all non-ASCII, and **square brackets** — is rejected at submission.
- **Placement**: the contract writes every field and instruction block itself, then splices `reason` last, between square-bracket fences that label it untrusted proposer text and mark where it ends. The charset is what makes those fences unforgeable, so the colon the URL set admits is defused structurally: no proposer string can close the fence, resume the contract's voice, or add a criterion.
- **Category**: a numbered argument, not part of the reason. The contract renders it into the claim as the one category the proposal claims, so the category is the proposer's declaration rather than the voters' inference, and no reason text can blur it. The claim tells voters the evidence must establish the declared category, and that grounds also fitting another category, such as a delisting that also ends the feed, do not invalidate it: every category is held to the same cutoff floor.

The listing claim applies the [same discipline](listing.md) to its `description`.

## The settlement window

Every path is **two-stage**. The terminator first *fixes* the settlement price (`fixSettlementPrice`), which opens the **settlement window**; `finalizeTermination` is allowed once **every position has been settled** at that price — the pair knows, because open interest reaches zero — or, as an escape hatch, **14 days** after the fix. Trading, orders, ADL, deposits, and **all** withdrawals (collateral and insurance alike) are frozen while the window is open, so nobody exits ahead of the accounting. The pair also stores no further price once the settlement price is fixed, so the funding index every settlement reads stays where the fix left it. A tick from before the cutoff is still fresh for three seconds after it, and storing one between two settlements would credit the positions settled after it with funding that those settled before it never paid, out of the insurers' residual.

The window exists to do one job: **mark every open position to the final price before anyone is paid.** `liquidate()` becomes the settlement entry point — anyone may pass a list of addresses, with no price update and no ETH, because the settlement price is already fixed (this is what makes it work on dead feeds). Settling is pure accounting and harmless to the target: it needs no solvency threshold, because marking a position at a price that will never change cannot alter what its owner is owed. Settling someone else's position earns a **$0.05** tip (`TERMINAL_SETTLEMENT_TIP`), escrowed for `claimFees()` like every keeper reward: the settled position pays it from whatever collateral its settlement leaves it, charged like a trading fee, and insurance tops up what the collateral cannot cover when the position is worth at least **$5** at the settlement price. A smaller position pays only what its own collateral covers: fills can leave positions that small with no collateral at no cost, so an insurance top-up would pay a tip out of the insurers' residual for every such account. Settling your own position is free. One batched call clears a book.

Each settled position does one of two things:

- **A loser's** realized loss is deducted from their collateral. Any loss exceeding their collateral is tallied as **bad debt**.
- **A winner's** profit is registered as a **claim**, the per-user figure paid at the frozen profit ratio.

Neither touches the deposits ledger. That is deliberate: the ledger obeys an identity the live protocol maintains at every step — the sum of every bucket's collateral, plus the open positions' unrealized PnL, plus the value of the vault's inherited inventory, equals the ledger less any ledger arrears plus any pre-termination deficit — and finalize derives what traders are owed from that identity. It holds only while settlement leaves the ledger alone.

Why any of this is necessary: liquidation only fires on observed live ticks, but termination events are precisely discontinuities — delistings, mergers, feed deaths. A position that gaps from healthy to deeply insolvent on the final print was never liquidatable at that price. Without a settlement pass, its bad debt is invisible, and winners get promised 100% of a pot that cannot pay them — a first-come-first-served drain.

Gating finalize on completeness rather than on a clock is what makes the payout exact, and the gate cannot be held hostage: nobody can withdraw until finalize, settling is permissionless and tipped, the order of settlement cannot change anyone's outcome, and settlement cannot revert for a position that exists. Every open position is a paying target for a keeper, and every winner with a payout waiting has a second reason to sweep the whole book rather than just their own position: a bankrupt position settled before finalize has its shortfall booked before the ratios are frozen, so it is charged to the insurers' residual, and shared across every registered winner at one ratio if that runs out; settled after finalize, the same shortfall can only be shared among the holders who have not withdrawn yet. The 14-day escape hatch only bounds the case where nobody settles at all.

## The settlement waterfall

At `finalizeTermination` the pair computes, once and for all:

1. **The total owed to traders**, from the ledger identity. With every position settled the unrealized term is zero, so `owed = ledger − ledger arrears + deficit + bad debt − inventory value at the settlement price`: the sum of every bucket's collateral plus every registered claim, without summing over accounts. The ledger arrears are what the deposits ledger owes the insurance fund — a seizure or vault gain the ledger could only partly pay because winners had already withdrawn the cash — so they are cash the ledger holds that no bucket claims; subtracting them keeps that cash out of the reserved principal and lets it reach the insurers' residual. The vault's inherited inventory is retired here — its loss is its counterparties' gain and its gain their loss, both already inside the buckets and claims.
2. **The pot**: the USDC balance less the fee escrow.
3. **Two frozen ratios.** Principal is senior: if the pot covers `owed − claims`, the principal ratio is 100% and the profit ratio is `min(100%, (pot − principal) / claims)`. If cash cannot even cover principal — only a books-vs-balance gap gets there — principal is paid pro-rata on the pot and profits pay nothing. Both ratios are stored at 18 decimals and rounded down, so a payout falls short of its exact share by about one wei per dollar. If positions finalize did not wait for are so far underwater that their negative equity exceeds the principal every other account holds (registered claims are not netted against it), principal is zero and the excess is carried; their settlement consumes that carry before crediting anything to the ledger, so only the part of a shortfall that offset real entitlements is ever credited.
4. **The insurance fund becomes the residual**: the pot less what the two ratios pay out on the totals. The ratios' rounding remainder (dust) therefore belongs to the insurers rather than sitting in the contract with no owner.

Withdrawals then pay **collateral × principal ratio + claim × profit ratio**. `withdrawCollateral` takes its amount in collateral units: the first withdrawal credits the registered claim at the profit ratio into collateral, and every withdrawal pays its amount times the principal ratio. `BazaarPairLens.getMaxWithdrawable` returns the largest amount it accepts, the same view as before termination, and `getTerminalEntitlement` the cash that amount pays. Three properties follow:

- **Principal is reserved at every moment**, with no deadline. Your deposit, minus your own trading losses, is never at risk from someone else's bankruptcy — and it doesn't expire if you never show up.
- **Insurance is junior to every trader.** Because the fund is whatever remains after traders are paid, it absorbs bad debt, the pre-termination deficit and the inventory's loss before any winner is cut, and inherits the inventory's gain — whether or not each item was tallied. The one exception is the settlement tip insurance tops up before finalize (up to `TERMINAL_SETTLEMENT_TIP`, $0.05, for a position worth at least $5 whose own collateral cannot pay it): it is credited to the fee escrow, which finalize takes off the pot first, so when the pot cannot pay every trader in full it reduces the traders' ratios rather than the insurers' residual.
- **Profit shortfalls are shared at one uniform percentage** across every registered winner, regardless of who withdraws first.

If finalize ran through the escape hatch, a position it did not wait for settles on its owner's first withdrawal (or when anyone settles it through `liquidate`). Its equity was already inside the total owed at collateral plus PnL, so a solvent position realizes to exactly that figure; a bankrupt one was counted below zero, so the insurers' residual funds its shortfall as far as it goes, and that funded amount moves into the ledger. Whatever the residual cannot fund is credited to the ledger as well, and both ratios are derived again, by the rule finalize used, on the entitlements still unpaid: the principal the ledger still owes, the registered claims not yet credited, and the cash the old ratios reserved for them (`TerminalRatiosRefrozen` reports the new ratios, and `getTerminalEntitlement` reads them). Everyone who has not withdrawn yet is then paid at the ratios the cash still in the contract supports, the fee escrow stays whole, and no withdrawal is refused for lack of cash. That is what finalize would have paid had the position been settled in time only if nobody withdrew in between: what earlier withdrawals took at the higher ratios cannot be recovered, so it falls on the holders still in, principal as well as profits — the case the completeness gate exists to prevent — but no withdrawal can take more than the ledger holds while a position is still open: that excess is the open positions' loss, which the ledger receives only when they settle, so it is refused with what the ledger can pay (`CollateralLib__LedgerShortfall`), and settling them releases the rest. Settling a bankrupt straggler after finalize earns the settler the same tip, from whatever residual its shortfall left (all-or-nothing, and only if the position was worth at least $5 at the settlement price); a solvent straggler pays none, since finalize already reserved its equity in full and settling it harms nobody.

Insurers cannot exit ahead of that settlement. After an escape-hatch finalize, `executeInsuranceWithdrawal` reverts (`InsuranceVaultLib__SweepIncomplete`) while any position is still open, until **14 days** beyond the escape hatch (`INSURER_SWEEP_GRACE`, so 28 days after the fix at the latest). Settling is permissionless, so an LP releases the gate by settling the remaining positions themselves, collecting the tip on any bankrupt one — an indexer lists them from `PositionBucketUpdated` events; the grace only bounds the case where nobody does.

A pair terminates exactly once; insurance LPs' withdrawal gates open at finalize — after an escape-hatch finalize, once the remaining positions are settled or the grace elapses — and their shares redeem against the residual. Finalize retires the vault's inventory and releases the collateral earmarked against it in the same step, so the residual is redeemable in full: with no inventory left to value, [share pricing](insurance.md) collapses to the residual over the supply, reads no mark, and needs no price update.

> **Note on inherited inventory.** The inventory is valued at the settlement price from the liquidated traders' original entry, **including the funding leg** — matching how the coverage ratio and the ADL gate value it. Dropping that leg would let the inventory's funding obligation vanish while its counterparties still collected theirs.
