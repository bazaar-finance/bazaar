# Earning on Bazaar

Two permissionless ways to earn from the protocol's operation, ordered by how much infrastructure they demand. (Building a frontend? That's its own page: [Integrators](integrators.md).)

## Insurance LP

**What it is:** deposit USDC into a specific market's insurance fund and hold shares of it. You are selling crash insurance on that one market — steady fee income against tail risk.

**You earn** a pro-rata slice of everything the fund keeps: insurance fees on every fill, forfeited governance bonds, and whatever the vault's inherited inventory returns above the collateral seized alongside it. That seizure is not income the moment it lands — it is earmarked against the position the vault just inherited and leaves again when that position closes, so what you keep is the part the close did not need.

**You risk** the fund's payouts: liquidation-unwind losses, ADL winner credits, keeper rewards — and in a market collapse, the fund is the last cushion before trader haircuts, so it can be drained. Shares are priced off the fund's value at the mark and float both ways: while the vault holds liquidated inventory, an exit is charged that inventory's unrealized loss (never paid its unrealized gain), and a deposit is priced at fair value on a fresh mark — you don't pay for a loss you didn't underwrite, and you don't buy a pending recovery at a discount. Details: [Insurance Fund](../protocol/insurance.md).

**The terms** (full detail: [Insurance Fund](../protocol/insurance.md)):

| Rule | Value |
|---|---|
| Minimum deposit | $5 — per market, choose which books you back. `depositToInsurance` takes a `priceUpdate`: while the vault holds liquidated inventory the deposit is priced at the mark and needs a fresh one, so on a closed venue it waits for the reopen; with the vault flat no price is read and an empty update works |
| Exit | request → **13-day cooldown** → 3-day execution window, in which you call `executeInsuranceWithdrawal` at least twice. The first call locks what your requested shares are worth and pays nothing; the second, from 5 minutes later (5 minutes after a price jump of 2% or more, 30 minutes after a market reopens), pays the lower of that and what they are worth then. While the pool holds liquidated positions it pays only as much as leaves its reserve and the cost of closing them covered wherever the price moves inside the unload band, at the pool's current target, and the rest stays requested for another call in the window. Either way a loss liquidators book in between is yours to share and a gain stays in the pool. A lock you never claim just lapses with the window. `BazaarPairLens.getInsuranceWithdrawalLock` tells you when your claim opens |
| Exit rate limits | slower when the fund is below target — you cannot stampede out of a thinning backstop; above target the budget's open-interest term counts only open interest the market has held for a full day, so positions opened and closed around a withdrawal do not widen it |
| Crisis lock | withdrawals blocked while the vault's liquidation exposure exceeds the ADL trigger (judged live by the execute call, against the price it fetches), when the fund has nothing left to pay from once its reserve and the cost of closing its liquidated inventory, taken together at whichever edge of the unload band makes them larger, are set aside (while it holds such inventory a payout is held to what is left, and a larger claim is paid that far with the rest left for later in the window, once the inventory has sold or the price has recovered), or from 6 s before a scheduled cutoff, or the fix on other paths, until finalize (the fund becomes the residual at finalize — LPs cannot exit ahead of it; a scheduled termination locks nothing before then, because the cutoff print exposes the fund to no loss a trading day would not); after an escape-hatch finalize, until the remaining positions are settled (you can settle them yourself through `liquidate`) or 14 days beyond the hatch. Deposits are refused while an ADL window is pending, or when the price they carry would open one, so the recovery an auction batch returns to the fund stays with the LPs who carried the risk |
| Market hours | the execute call needs a fresh oracle price (≤ 3 s old), so on non-24/7 markets exits happen during trading hours — the frozen closing mark is refused; a market whose feed has died releases LPs once the 21-day stale termination settles it. Deposits face the same gate only while the vault holds liquidated inventory, since that is the only case where a deposit has to price it |
| Governance | matured shares (4 days, the listing seed included) propose and vote on shutting the market down (60% threshold) |

The honest framing: you get paid *because* your capital is locked in exactly the moments you'd want it out. Size accordingly.

## Keeper bounties

**What it is:** run a bot that calls permissionless maintenance functions and collects the bounty attached to each. No bond, no stake — just gas and uptime. All bounties are USDC, credited to your address's claimable balance on the pair the moment you earn them and withdrawn in one `claimFees()` call per pair — most are funded from the pair's insurance fund (capped at its free capital — never the seizure earmarked against inventory the vault still holds); trigger bounties were escrowed from the serviced order's owner when it was placed, and settlement tips come from the settled position's collateral first, with insurance topping up only for positions worth at least $5.

| Opportunity | Call | Bounty | What your bot watches |
|---|---|---|---|
| Liquidations | `liquidate(users[], priceUpdate)` | $0.05 for each position seized or deferred liquidation newly recorded, or max($0.05, 2 bp of the notional it retires) for a seizure that nets against the vault's inventory, plus whatever 2 bp of what the call's inline unload sells into the book exceeds those rewards beyond the nettings' 2 bp; an account whose position is worth less than $5 counts for at most its own equity instead of $0.05 — batching many accounts into one call earns the same as splitting them, for less gas; a call that only re-submits already-listed accounts earns just the 2 bp on anything its unload sells | position health vs. lagged MMR (`checkBucketSolvency` on the lens); carry a `priceUpdate` on a closed market — a stale stored price older than 10 min cannot seize collateral. Re-submitting accounts already listed in `deferredLiquidations` is worth it whenever the fund has grown: the call seizes them the moment their netting fits, and each netting earns its 2 bp |
| Trigger execution | `executeTriggeredOrders(execs[], priceUpdate)` | the bounty the owner attached to the order, escrowed at creation: paid in proportion to the size your call fills out of what remained, the whole remainder once the order completes or rests; nothing for an execution in the creation block of an order the stale fallback left unarmed, which pays the owner; ≤ 20 per call | untriggered stops' trigger prices vs. the live Pyth price, and their escrowed bounties (`TriggerBountyEscrowed`, less any `TriggerBountyReleased`, or the lens's `triggerBounty(pair, orderId)`); a fired order the book could not finish stays live and is worth its unpaid remainder |
| Vault unloading | `unloadVault(maxSteps, priceUpdate)` | 2 bp of unloaded notional always; floor of $0.25 when ≥ $1,250 unloads or the call clears the inventory | `getPendingLiquidationExposure` on the lens after liquidations. Carry a fresh `priceUpdate`: against the stale fallback the call fills nothing and pays nothing. Bids inside min(5%, MMR) of oracle fill immediately; a bid beyond that edge but above the estates' bankruptcy price fills only once the vault's floor has slid down to it, which takes up to 3 minutes from the returned seizure stamp |
| Price refresh | `refreshPrice(priceUpdate)` | $0.10 when the stored price is > 5 min old while the vault holds ≥ $1,250 of inventory, or > 1 day old otherwise, on pairs holding > $5k of free insurance capital | quiet pairs' last stored-price timestamps — the refresh also judges vault health, so on a book too thin to trade it is what opens an ADL window (and starts its 24 h clock) once inherited inventory drifts past the trigger |
| ADL execution | `executeAdl(winners[], deferredWinners[], priceUpdate)` | 0.1% of averted bad debt across both cohorts, plus max($0.05, 2 bp at the mark of the notional the batch retires with it) for each deferred account the batch seizes — $0.05 alone for one it moves into the inventory — the $0.05 capped at the account's own equity when its position is worth less than $5 | `isAdlPending`; `winners` is the side opposite the vault's inventory — sort it by `getAdlScore` on the lens, descending with ties by ascending address, which reproduces that cohort's on-chain ranking exactly, and a mis-sorted or duplicated entry among those the batch admits reverts. `deferredWinners` is the inventory's own side, submitted when `auxState().deferredCount` is non-zero; it is ranked on-chain against the price your transaction stores rather than the window's snapshot, and the lens scores it at the pair's last stored price. That price is Pyth's cached tick when the cache is fresh, your update only otherwise, so score this cohort at the price your transaction will store (simulate `refreshPrice(priceUpdate)` then the lens reads in one `eth_call`), and if a newer tick lands first and the batch reverts out of order, re-score and resubmit. Either list may be empty, not both |
| Dead-market cleanup | `terminateStalePair`, `terminateScheduledPair`, UMA termination proposals | nothing for the two direct calls; a UMA termination proposal (`proposeTermination`, which posts a bond) earns its proposer 0.1% (10 bp) of the fund once its acceptance schedules the pair (an acceptance that only caps an earlier insurer cutoff pays nothing), once per pair, capped $100 and at the fund's free capital | oracle silence > 21 days; scheduled cutoffs that have passed — `settlementRule(pair)` on the terminator says what `terminateScheduledPair` needs: under UMA's rule one Pyth print, the first in the window ending at the cutoff; under the chain rule for an insurer vote UMA has not upheld, five prints chained ten minutes apart from the cutoff, fetched in sequence ([how](../protocol/termination.md#supplying-the-settlement-prints)) |
| Terminal settlement | `liquidate` (settlement mode) while the settlement window is open, then `finalizeTermination` | $0.05 per position settled for someone else (`TERMINAL_SETTLEMENT_TIP`): from the position's remaining collateral, insurance topping up only for a position worth at least $5 at the settlement price, so a smaller one pays just what its collateral covers; after an escape-hatch finalize only bankrupt stragglers of at least $5 pay, from the insurers' residual | a pair whose settlement price is fixed — every open position, listed from `PositionBucketUpdated` events; needs no `priceUpdate` and no ETH |

Practical notes: every price-touching call takes a `priceUpdate` — your bot pushes the Pyth update and acts on it atomically (Pyth's fee is paid in ETH, refunded when unused). Bounties are never pushed to you: watch `FeeAccrued` events or ask the lens (`getClaimableFeesAll(you)` lists the balance on every pair in one call), and call `claimFees()` whenever a balance is worth the gas — one claim per pair sweeps every bounty kind, and it works in every pair state, including after termination. Liquidation and trigger-execution markets are competitive — latency matters; unloading, ADL, and termination cleanup are sleepier niches.
